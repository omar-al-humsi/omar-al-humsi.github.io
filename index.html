Job Market Paper

Labor Taxes as Investment Policy: Optimal Income Taxation with Union Hold-Up

Single-authored

Paper (PDF)

I study optimal redistributive taxation when unions bargain over wages after firms have sunk their capital. Collective bargaining then creates two distortions: wages above market-clearing levels ration jobs, and unions appropriate part of the return to sunk capital, so firms underinvest. Because the firm-specific capital exposed to this hold-up cannot be verified by the tax authority, it cannot be subsidized directly; taxes on work and unemployment benefits affect investment only through equilibrium wages and employment. I derive a sufficient-statistics formula for optimal participation taxes in which the standard equity–efficiency trade-off gains an investment term: the social value of capital times the response of investment to the tax. A higher tax on work raises investment if and only if it reduces unions’ claims on the return to capital by more than it reduces capital’s productivity, a condition that depends only on labor-market responses. In a calibration to France, hold-up reduces the optimal participation subsidy for low-wage work relative to a unionized economy without hold-up, while leaving the taxation of high-wage work essentially unchanged.

Presented at Public Economic Theory Conference (PET), Lisbon, 2025 · Journées Louis-André Gérard-Varet (LAGV), Marseille, 2025 · CNRS Thematic School on Public Policy Evaluation (ETEPP), Paris, 2025

Other Research

Working Paper

Redistributive Income Taxation, Directed Technical Change, and Union Wage Bargaining

Single-authored · Presented at the LMU Munich Public Economics Seminar, 2026

This paper studies optimal redistributive income taxation when wages are set through union bargaining and the direction of technical change is endogenous. It characterizes how income taxes steer innovation and, through it, compress pre-tax wage inequality.
