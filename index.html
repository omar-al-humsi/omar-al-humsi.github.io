Job Market Paper

Labor Taxes as Investment Policy: Optimal Income Taxation with Union Hold-Up

When wages rise with installed capital, labor income taxes change how much of the return to additional capital firms retain. I characterize optimal redistributive income taxation when firms invest before wages are collectively bargained and capital cannot be taxed or subsidized directly. A higher participation tax raises the negotiated wage at given capital but can lower capital-to-wage pass-through, the wage increase generated by additional capital, and thereby raise investment. The optimal participation tax therefore includes an investment effect: the investment response valued at the marginal social value of capital. In a calibration to France, letting firms anticipate that capital raises negotiated wages offsets about nine-tenths of the reduction in optimal participation tax rates that collective bargaining would otherwise imply.

Presented at Public Economic Theory Conference (PET), Lisbon, 2025 · Journées Louis-André Gérard-Varet (LAGV), Marseille, 2025 · CNRS Thematic School on Public Policy Evaluation (ETEPP), Paris, 2025

Redistributive Income Taxation, Directed Technical Change, and Union Wage Bargaining

Working paper · Presented at the LMU Munich Public Economics Seminar, 2026

This paper studies optimal redistributive income taxation when wages are set through union bargaining and the direction of technical change is endogenous. It characterizes how income taxes steer innovation and, through it, compress pre-tax wage inequality.
